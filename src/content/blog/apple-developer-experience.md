Patience is a virtue, but not one of mine.

Like every other member of the Apple Developer program, I willingly hand over my $99 per year, plus 15% of all earnings from in-app purchases. In return, Apple is expected to provide several things: distribution on the App Store, a reasonable review process, access to TestFlight for QA, and developer support.

The experience I've faced with my newest app submission serves as a perfect microcosm of what the developer experience has become.
## Reasonable Review Process

As of this very moment, it is October 18th, 1:48 PM ET. I submitted my app for review on October 14th, at 12:44 am, so let's say the evening of the 13th. That means it has been 110 hours since my app was submitted.

Apple's own guidelines state that it reviews 50% of submissions in less than 24 hours and 90% in less than 48 hours. A quick Reddit search shows that mine is not an isolated case. Apple simply no longer lives up to their promised standards and it's unacceptable.

I understand that Apple is no doubt being bombarded by AI slop submissions and their current review team is likely overwhelmed by the queue length. Though, as the most profitable company in the world, there is no excuse to fall behind based on sheer workforce. If you need to hire more people to carry the load, then do it.

## Access to TestFlight

TestFlight straight up refused to work or my newest app. Whenever I tried to install the build on my phone, I was greeted with an error message stating that the app could not be installed. 

After searching for a possible cause, it became clear that this was an Apple backend issue, and a common one at that. The only way to fix this issue is to contact Apple Developer support and have them fix it on their end, so that's exactly what I did. We'll get more into that a little later.

As a result, I was forced to test my app through an ad hoc method. Not the biggest issue, but still a pain in the ass.

## Developer Support

Over the past two weeks I have opened up two separate tickets for unrelated issues and I have received zero response. None.

My first attempt was due to the TestFlight issue I mentioned earlier. Due to this being an issue that only Developer support could solve, the lack of response was extremely disheartening. What if I didn't know what ad hoc testing was even a thing? Would I just be stuck?

My second attempt was two days ago, just trying to get a better understanding on the timeline for app approvals. Not an unreasonable request. As you might have guessed, complete silence.

## What Are We Paying For?

Let's take a look back at the expectations that Apple sets for Apple Developers:

1. **Distribution on the App Store**: Complete failure here, as I can't even get my app through the first step of the approval process.
2. **Reasonable Review Process**: Based on their own guidelines, they are failing miserably.
3. **Access to TestFlight**: Again, big zero here as well. Due to their common backend issues, many developers, including me, are unable to natively QA their apps.
4. **Developer Support**: I'm still yet to receive a single response. Even stupid questions should get a response.

Apple is failing on all fronts, and so far, it seems everyone is just dealing with it, not that there's much of a choice. If Apple gives a single shit about the people who have made them obnoxiously cash rich, they need to do the right thing and invest more in the developer experience. Anything less is pure negligence.
